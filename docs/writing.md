# How we write about WallFin

Goal: anyone — a student, a customer, a curious neighbour — should enjoy our
docs and leave smarter than they arrived. Writing like that is a craft. Here
is ours, in ten small rules.

1. **Talk to one person.** "You can watch the pad settle" — not "the user can
   observe the behaviour of the system."
2. **Wonder, not war.** Real walls are interesting puzzles, not enemies. No
   battle words: no "adversarial", "beats", "traps", "fails". Say what is
   true, warmly: "Real walls are never perfectly flat."
3. **Explain the why.** A fact earns its place by teaching. Don't just say
   "soft contact" — say what softness buys: forgiveness for small mistakes.
4. **Paint a picture in the mind.** "5 N is a water bottle resting in your
   palm." "A wide pad is a beach ball: tilt it, and it still touches at one
   spot."
5. **Short sentences, warm rhythm.** One idea each. Follow a long sentence
   with a short one. Let the reader breathe.
6. **Honest, kindly.** "What we're still learning" reads better than "risk
   list". Say hard truths plainly and gently, then point to the fix.
7. **Show the workshop, don't defend a thesis.** "We built this, you can try
   it." Invite the reader in — no marketing armour.
8. **Numbers with meaning.** A number alone is noise. Give it a friend:
   "94 % — about nine touches in ten land with the right pressure."
9. **Be generous with others.** Respect what other machines do well. Explain
   how we differ without putting anyone down.
10. **End with a door.** Every doc finishes with a next step: run it, watch
    the video, tell us what you think.

## Before → after

- Before: "The wall is not flat on purpose. It has three traps."
- After: "Real walls are never flat. Ours carries three of the surprises you
  meet in actual rooms."

- Before: "The fixed-depth run fails the other way."
- After: "Without force feedback, the same wall tells a different story."

- Before: "Moat and direction."
- After: "The edge, and where we're headed."
